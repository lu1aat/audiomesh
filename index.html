    How it works, step by step → Eleven short chapters with demos that run this app's own code, from the bits in a frame to a decode lab with the real receiver. Your station keeps running while you read.

    Send your first message

    audiomesh sends text from one device's speaker to another device's microphone. No internet, no account. Three steps, and every setting stays on its default.

    1. A

      Switch on

      In the sidebar, press Audio, then TX. Allow the microphone when the browser asks. Both lamps light up: you are listening, and you may send.

      A yellow bar asks for a name? Press Set your name and type up to 8 letters, so other stations see a name instead of a number.

      Put the devices a few metres apart, volume about half, nothing loud nearby.

    2. B

      Press Test, with another station

      Ask someone nearby to do step A on their device too. Then, on Network, both press Test. Each device sends a short beep-like signal and the other answers it. After a minute or two you appear in each other's list.

      No other station at hand? Skip to C. A message to Public goes out anyway; nobody confirms it.

    3. C

      Write and send

      Open Messages. Tap a station in the list (or Public to speak to everyone), type, and press Send at next slot.

      The message leaves at the next 15-second slot. A short one (up to 7 characters) takes one slot, a full 142 characters about 4 minutes. A ✓✓ delivered under your message means the other station got all of it.

    Text is shown in upper case with a limited set of symbols. That is how it fits so few sound frames.

    Choose a mode

    The default is Normal. Slower modes reach further and work in more noise. Faster modes send more, but need a good signal and clocks that agree closely. Both stations must use the same mode, or they cannot hear each other at all. Change it on Network in Network options; the page reloads.

    Unsure? Fast in the same room, Normal for anything farther, Long or Deep for the weakest signals. Turbo is a testing tool for devices very close; Spread and Burst are variants of Normal for difficult rooms.

    Compare the modes: speed, width, clock and reach →

    Choose a band

    The band is the range of pitches the signal uses. The default is ultrasonic: inaudible, and the most channels. Both stations need the same band too.

    • Ultrasonic (17.5 to 21 kHz): silent to most people. Needs a 48 kHz sound card and a speaker and microphone that reach that high.
    • Audible: works on any hardware, but you will hear it.
    • Low (100 to 300 Hz): a narrow band for special cases.

    See each band's channels in every mode, and what your sound card can carry →

    Talk to other stations

    Stations appear when you hear their Test or Announce. The app also sends one every few minutes by itself, so a station you have not met yet turns up on its own.

    To everyone

    Pick Public. Everyone close enough hears it. It is not confirmed: you will not know who got it.

    To one station

    Tap its name in the list. It answers with a small "got it", lost pieces are sent again by themselves, and the message shows how far it got:

    Draw a picture

    In a conversation, press Sprite. Paint a small picture (up to 16 by 16 squares, a few colours) or pick one from the gallery, and send it. It appears on the other device while it arrives: coarse first, sharper with every frame.

    What each screen shows

    • Messages: the list of stations on the left (Public on top), the open conversation, Send, Announce and the sprite editor.
    • Network: the map of stations (a stronger link is a shorter line), channels and waterfall, the station list with signal and time since last heard, signal per channel, clocks, and every frame sent or received.
    • Settings: your name and icon, notifications, sprite display and stored data.

    Network options

    These sit on Network, under Network options. The defaults suit most people. The tiles below show each option as it comes out of the box.

    Audio
    Listen to the microphone. Off, the station is deaf and silent. It is the same switch as the Audio key in the sidebar.
    Allow transmit
    The master switch for everything you send. Off, nothing goes out, not even a reply: the station only listens.
    Auto channel
    Picks the best channel for each message from how well each station hears you. Off, everything goes on the channel you selected.
    Auto beacon
    Every few minutes, sends a beacon (your name if nobody is heard yet, link reports otherwise) and answers a newly heard station. It keeps the map fresh. With many stations, choose a longer interval.
    Repeater
    Repeats what it hears, so stations that cannot hear each other can still talk through you. Everyone then sends their frames three slots apart, so messages take about three times as long.
    Deep decode
    Searches further in time, for a station whose clock is wrong: 3 seconds more each side on top of the usual 2 in Normal (less in Fast and Turbo, whose slots are short). Costs about a third more processing.

    Test sends one beacon that every station hearing it answers, so you see who hears you and how well. Announce sends your name and icon. Reset to defaults (press twice) puts these options back; Audio, your name and timing stay.

    Clocks and timing

    Slots start at the same second on every station, so every clock must be close to right. A receiver tolerates about 2 seconds of error in Normal, less in the faster modes (under half a second in Turbo). If you hear frames but nothing decodes, a clock is probably off.

    Move a clock and watch the frame fall out of the window, with today's slots live →

    Open Network, then the Sync section. Its dot says how things stand: green is fine, yellow means an offset is applied, red means your clock is off against the server.

    • Check against server, then Use server time, aligns you with the clock of the server this page came from. The page checks by itself at startup and every 10 minutes, and shows a warning bar when the difference is large.
    • Sync to this station, on a station row, copies the timing of a station you do hear.
    • A manual offset in seconds, and Back to UTC to undo any of these.
    • The hints in the section say whether your clock or theirs seems to be off.

    When something goes wrong

    Nobody appears

    Check that both stations use the same band and the same mode, that Audio is on, and that one of you is sending (press Test). Get closer and turn the volume up.

    Frames are heard but do not decode, or arrive at the wrong time

    The clocks are out of sync. Use the Sync section on Network (see Clocks and timing above): check against the server, or sync to a station you do hear.

    The signal is there but nothing decodes

    Check the microphone level (a channel shown in yellow is overloaded), get closer, or move both stations to a slower mode.

    On a phone

    Phone clocks drift, so sync them. The browser stops audio when the screen turns off or the tab is in the background: keep the page open and the screen on. A Bluetooth headset can force a low sample rate and make the ultrasonic band unavailable; use the phone's own speaker and microphone. The microphone needs the page to be on https (or localhost). If the speaker and microphone sit at the bottom edge, use Rotate 180° in the sidebar to point them at the other station.

    The page reloads in the language you pick: turn Audio on again afterwards. How it works opens in the same language.

    Shows a system notification for a new message or an announcement while this page is in the background.

    Off by default. When on, this station connects to a public MQTT broker (broker.emqx.io) and publishes one small record per frame it sends or hears: its station id and name, band and mode, channel, signal, frame type and who it was from and to. A new Stats screen shows what all the stations that do this have heard. Never audio, never message text or pictures. This is the only connection to another server besides the clock check.

    The modes switched on here are the ones the Protocol selector on the Network screen offers. A packet is one frame on the air; a message goes out one packet per slot, 7 characters in the first and 9 in each further one, so its time grows with its length (directed messages also wait for the ack). The mode in use stays on. Both stations must use the same mode.

    Every channel decodes the whole band each slot. Off, only the selected channel is decoded, which uses less CPU. Decode while sending also hears our own frames, which is how to check the timing (their dt should be about 0).

    Sends your name at that interval while audio is on and a channel is selected.

    Adds a Capture section to the Network screen. Capturing keeps the audio of receive windows in memory, to replay a failed decode offline; it starts only when you press Start capture there, and stops after 30 minutes. Nothing leaves this browser until you press Download. Switching this off stops and clears any capture.

    On a phone the speaker and microphone are at the bottom edge. Rotating the screen 180° lets you hold the phone upside down, pointing them at the other stations. The Rotate 180° button in the sidebar does the same.

    The colour map and the lines drawn over the waterfall. Red (our frame), yellow (input overloaded) and blue (selected) keep their meaning on every theme. Rows already on screen keep their old colours until they scroll off.

    Pixel size is how large one sprite pixel is drawn in the messages and in the editor (wide sprites shrink to fit a narrow screen). Missing pixels are those of a partly received sprite.
