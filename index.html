    Help

    audiomesh sends short text messages between devices using only a speaker and a microphone. No internet, no server: your device plays the signal, the other one hears it. It is slow on purpose, so it can work far away and in noise.

    Set up your station

    1. Set your name. Open Settings, find Station name and type a name of up to 8 characters. (The yellow "Set your name" bar at the top takes you there.) Without a name, other stations only see a random id. The name is sent when you press Announce.
    2. Turn the station on. Open Network, then Network options. Switch Audio on (the browser asks for the microphone) and Allow transmit on. The Audio and TX lamps at the top show the state.
    3. Choose band and mode in the same panel. Both stations must use the same band and the same mode, or they cannot hear each other at all. See the table below.

    Choose a mode

    Slower modes reach further and work in more noise; faster modes send more but need a good signal and closer clocks. 142 chars is the time for one full message (16 frames, one per slot). Down to is the weakest signal that still decodes reliably (dB in a 2500 Hz noise bandwidth).

    ModeSlot142 charsDown toBest for
    D Deep60 s16 min-23 dBLongest range, weakest signals
    L Long30 s8 min-21 dBLong range
    A Normal15 s4 min-18 dBGeneral use
    B Medium10 s2 min 40 s-14 dBFaster, medium distance
    C Fast5 s1 min 20 s-11 dBDevices nearby (same room): recommended
    T Turbo2.5 s40 s-9 dBFastest; a testing tool for devices very close

    If you are unsure, use C for devices in the same room and A for anything farther. Changing the mode reloads the page.

    Band: ultrasonic (17.5 to 21 kHz) is inaudible and has the most channels, so it is a good default when your speakers and microphones reach it (it needs a 48 kHz sound card). Audible works with any hardware but you will hear it. Low (100 to 300 Hz) is a narrow band for special cases.

    Talk to other stations

    1. Listen first. With Audio on, wait to see stations appear on Network and in the list on Messages. They show up when their test beacons or announcements are heard, which by default happens every few minutes. Pressing Test and Announce on both stations speeds this up.
    2. Write to everyone. Once you see stations, open Messages, leave the recipient on Everyone and send. This is not confirmed: you will not know who received it.
    3. Write to one station. After you announce yourself, or receive an announcement from another station, that station appears in the recipient list (or click it in the list on the left of Messages). A directed message is confirmed: the other station sends an acknowledgement, lost parts are sent again, and the message shows as delivered or failed.

    Messages are up to 142 characters. Text is converted to upper case and a limited set of symbols. Frames go out one per slot, so be patient.

    What each screen shows

    • Messages: the list of stations (Public on top for messages addressed to nobody; click one to open its conversation), the conversation, Send, Announce and the sprite editor.
    • Network: the map of stations (a stronger link is a shorter line), the channels and waterfall, the list of stations with signal and time since last heard, signal per channel, timing (Sync) and every frame sent or received.
    • Settings: your name, notifications, sprite display and stored data.
    • Colours show age: green heard in the last minute, then orange and red, grey after ten minutes.

    Network options

    • Audio: listen to the microphone. Off, the station is deaf and silent.
    • Allow transmit: master switch for everything you send. Off, nothing goes out.
    • Test: sends a beacon that asks every other station to answer, for a quick picture of who hears you and how well. Announce: sends your name.
    • Auto channel: picks the best channel for each message from the link quality. Auto beacon: every few minutes sends a beacon (your name if nobody is heard yet, link reports otherwise), and answers a newly heard station, to keep that picture fresh.
    • Repeater: repeats what it hears from others, so stations that cannot hear each other can still talk. Deep decode: also looks for frames that arrive further off in time.
    • Reset to defaults: puts these options back (Audio, your name and timing are kept).

    Troubleshooting

    • Nobody appears. Check that both stations use the same band and the same mode, that Audio is on, and that one of them is sending (press Test). Get closer and turn the volume up.
    • Frames are heard but do not decode, or arrive at the wrong time. The clocks are out of sync. Transmissions happen in slots aligned to UTC and the receiver tolerates only a small error (about 2 seconds in mode A, less in faster modes, under half a second in T). Use the Sync section on Network:
      • Check against server, then Use server time, to align with the clock of the server this page came from. The page checks it by itself at startup and every 10 minutes, and shows a warning bar if the difference is large.
      • Sync to this station, on a station row, to copy the timing of a station you do hear.
      • A manual offset in seconds, and Back to UTC to undo any of these.
      • The hints in the Sync section say whether your clock or theirs seems to be off.
    • On a phone. Phone clocks drift, so sync them. The browser stops audio when the screen turns off or the tab is in the background: keep the page open and the screen on. A Bluetooth headset can force a low sample rate and make the ultrasonic band unavailable; use the phone's own speaker and microphone. The microphone needs the page to be on https (or localhost).
    • Signal heard but nothing decodes. Check the microphone level (a channel shown in yellow is overloaded), get closer, or move both stations to a slower mode.
